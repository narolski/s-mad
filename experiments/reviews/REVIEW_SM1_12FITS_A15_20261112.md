## Findings, most severe first

**F1 (major, mis-aimed conclusion): A15 does not show that history crossing removes diffusion detection.**
Using the SAME20 rung (registered as descriptive; my reading is post hoc):

| Contrast | a | e | f | a − e |
|---|---|---|---|---|

- X20 against SAME20 is the contrast that controls for the second person: both are mixes, and only the partner's identity differs.
- What arm a loses under history crossing is "any mix over a reconstruction", one-person and two-person alike, by the same amount (+0.25). It is not a second-identity signal.
- On the identity contrast every arm sits at about 0.65. That is below one sharpness number (face-box Laplacian, X vs SAME, oriented 0.695; source: FZ2 read_addon_items.csv). Within Laplacian quintiles all arms fall to 0.59–0.63 (a 0.603 / 0.587, e 0.632 / 0.610).

**F2 (major, answer to question 3): arm a's 0.745 is not the measured softness or file-size difference.**
- X20 is slightly sharper and larger than REC20: Laplacian AUC 0.582, PNG bytes 0.711.
- Arm a's score correlates negatively with both inside each class. Spearman ρ with log Laplacian: within X −0.30 / −0.33, within REC −0.20 / −0.33.
- Removing log Laplacian and log PNG size from a's score raises its AUC (0.733 / 0.828) rather than lowering it. Laplacian-stratified: 0.729 / 0.815.
- So arm a reads a render-path property of mixed (slerped, not the photo's own) latents that is not face-box sharpness or file size. That fits FZ2 F5: any image not decoded from the photo's own noise map is separable from its reconstruction.

## Question 6: are these recipes detecting diffusion morphs or the render path?

On the evidence so far, these recipes detect diffusion morphs by the render path, softness or history. Nothing detected depends on a second identity.

**For:**
- AD4: networks trained without any morph (softness only) reach MorDIFF EER 0.0% native and 2.9% history-matched. That is better than any morph-trained SM1 arm.
- SM1 standard recipe: the diffusion cells rise +18 to +22 points under storage history.
- e and f (history crossing): the rise is gone, and MorDIFF native EER is 36–40%.
- A15: on the identity contrast X vs SAME, a = e = f ≈ 0.65, below one sharpness statistic and about 0.60 within Laplacian strata.
- FZ2: two-person mixes sit with reconstructions in the frozen features.
- PN1: transfer of X vs REC is 0.47–0.79, against a floor of 0.650.

**Against:**
- Arm a's mix signal is not explained by the two simple cues measured.
- A residual of about 0.60 on X vs SAME remains within strata in every arm. It has no interval yet.
- Only one generator, 536 hosts and 2 seeds.

**What to expect:** a shortcut-free single-image method trained on SMDD-type data should be expected to score near chance on render-matched diffusion morphs. Arm e gives MorDIFF history-matched EER of 23–32% and native 34–47%. Published low MorDIFF EERs are consistent with render-path detection.

So the answer to the author's question ("we found the shortcuts, why can't we train so we do not learn them") is that, for diffusion morphs, the training data give these recipes no other signal to learn. This is not a regularisation failure.

## Risks to the story

- A sentence implying that the remedy "costs diffusion detection" invites the reply "your remedy breaks the detector". The evidence says there was no identity-level diffusion detection to lose.
- Frame it as: "on SMDD-type training, single-image diffusion-morph detection is render-path detection; removing the path leaves chance-level performance on render-matched morphs."
- Caveat to state alongside it: one generator, 2 seeds.
- Keep AR1 and DG1 designated as development sets in writing. AR1 is also used to audit released detectors, so state that those AR1 numbers are audits, not benchmarks of our method.
