## 1. Verdict

**Needs qualification; conclusions 2 and 3 do not follow as worded.**

- **Conclusion 1 follows** from the registered rule.
- **Conclusion 2 reads the ladder backwards**, and calls a calculation on landmark files a signal.
- **Conclusion 3 rests on a statistic that cannot see even an ideal midpoint** in face-recognition features, and on a "ceiling" that is not one.
- **Conclusion 4 is right in its core but over-general.**

**Close the line as a detector component and as a ceiling claim.** Nothing here supports "the second identity leaves little in one image" or "benchmark error rates must come from process traces". ID0 only rules out typicality (closeness to the average face) as their source.

## 2. Findings, most severe first

**F1 (blocker for conclusion 3): the face-recognition statistic has no power, so "at chance" and "clean control" are both uninformative.**
- On ideal genuine-only midpoints the registered score gives 0.521, and 0.353 in the top similarity third (`arm_glint.json`, `virtual_in_domain`).
- Reviewer check: on ideal normalised midpoints of the ladder's own pairs it gives 0.44 to 0.46.
- The cited 0.503 / 0.554 / 0.545 therefore say nothing about morphs.
- The powered read-outs in the same file do see landmark morphs:

| Read-out | Contrast | Value |
|---|---|---|
| Virtual head | warp-only against two-person morph | 0.590 [0.546, 0.631] |
| Virtual head | same-person against two-person | 0.557 [0.514, 0.598] |
| Mean-direction gain | OpenCV two-person morph | 1.108 [1.057, 1.157] |

- The head's own control fails on the MorDIFF history: 0.415 [0.396, 0.427]. So no arm is both powered and clean.
- Reviewer check: an ideal midpoint of the same ladder pairs scores only 0.64 with the head and 0.61 on the mean direction. Real OpenCV morphs reach 0.59 and 0.54; FaceMorpher 0.58 and 0.50.
- So in face-recognition features the effect is small because the ideal is small, and real landmark morphs realise more than half of it.
- Diffusion: 0.554 [0.471, 0.640], gain 1.01 [0.81, 1.21]. This is "not shown", not "absent".
- StyleGAN3: 0.545 [0.513, 0.578] is above chance. The head gives 0.600 against a contributor render but 0.458 against spread-matched renders, so it reads latent typicality, which a single render shares.

Rewrite: "In face-recognition features an ideal two-person midpoint is only slightly more typical than a photo (0.61 to 0.64 on the ladder pairs); real landmark morphs reach 0.54 to 0.59, diffusion morphs are not distinguishable from reconstructions within wide intervals. Typicality cannot explain near-zero benchmark errors. What a second identity leaves in an image is not measured here."

**F2 (major): the "ceiling" is not a ceiling.**
- The 0.784 uses Λ = 2.68 (Ledoit-Wolf). The ridge variants in the same JSON (3.70 to 4.81) give 0.86 to 0.92.
- Reviewer check, same held-out fold of 156 persons: the head's in-domain value still climbs with fit persons.

| Fit persons | 75 | 150 | 300 | 620 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Morph against single photo | 0.588 | 0.605 | 0.648 | 0.733 |

- Weaker regularisation (C = 10) gives 0.758.
- It is also a linear, first-moment bound in one feature space.
- Fix: strike "ceiling"; write "what a linear head reaches with 776 persons".

**F3 (major): conclusion 2 is inverted, and the landmark arm is arithmetic.**
- No morph image is read. The positive class is the mean of two source landmark files.
- "Dose monotone, p = 4e-234" is algebra: a quadratic score on a linear interpolation.
- The ladder shows the opposite of "averaging in general":

| Contrast (exact shapes) | Value |
|---|---|
| Photo against cross-session same-person mean | 0.548 [0.536, 0.559] |
| Photo against own-mirror mean | 0.572 |
| Same-person mean against two-person mean | 0.687 [0.650, 0.720] |

- All 536 host pairs are cross-session (`experiments/AR1/pool.csv`), so pose and session averaging are bounded by the 0.548. Most of the effect is specific to the second person.
- "Closed form within 0.03" holds only with the pool-centred similarity added after the numbers were seen. The registered estimator misses by 0.04 on the ladder (0.687 against 0.725) and by 0.10 to 0.11 on benchmark pairs (0.634 against 0.737; 0.650 against 0.757).
- The ten benchmark-pair rows are two results repeated; the families share pair lists.

Rewrite: "Computed from exact landmark files, the mean shape of two persons is more average than a real shape (0.725 [0.689, 0.758]); the mean of two sessions of one person hardly is (0.548). Whether this can be read from a morph image is untested."

**F4 (major): conclusion 4 is over-general, and the informative control is the same-person rung.**
- Only the MorDIFF-size history fails. The StyleGAN copies are identical to native (cosine 1.000), so there are three informative families.
- CLIP's 0.582 [0.543, 0.621] sits inside the original band, and its sign flips with the score variant (total typicality 0.377).
- For within-pipeline contrasts the valid control is the same-person mix:

| Encoder | Same-person mix against two-person morph | Reconstruction against same-person mix | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|
| CLIP | 0.419 | 0.756 | reads "two codes mixed" |
| DINOv3 | 0.478 | 0.627 | reads "two codes mixed" |
| Diffusion autoencoder | 0.747 | 0.445 | passes |

- The diffusion autoencoder also passes on the ladder (same-person against two-person 0.676) and on the landmark-family history controls (0.515, 0.514).
- What disqualifies it is that its absolute level follows frame and pool: MorDIFF history 0.000, stored crops 0.462 against native 0.688, cross-pool 0.004.
- Reviewer check: ideal feature midpoints of the evaluation pools would score 0.83 to 0.90 in all three encoders. Real images score 0.69 (diffusion autoencoder), 0.53 (DINOv3) and 0.36 (CLIP). The limit is that the feature of a blended image is not the blend of the features, not history "first".
- The diffusion-autoencoder shift is only weakly related to the exact-shape shift per host (rho +0.23, n = 520).

## 3. What this means for the mirror-asymmetry test (AS0)

- ID0 does not kill it. Symmetry and typicality are different axes: the own-mirror shape is barely more typical (0.572).
- Its exact-shape asymmetry numbers will be arithmetic, as here. Only the image bands test the idea.
- Expect the same weak zone. ID0's best ideal case for "one variance component halves, read without a reference" was 0.69 to 0.73, and images realised about half.
- Its self-referenced ratio avoids ID0's pool-offset problem, and it already has a positive control and a cross-session rung. Keep both decisive.
