## Findings (most severe first)

**1. MAJOR: the claim that "R4 vs R3 is trained by none of the three arms" is false.** Source: prereg.md:490-491 and :519-520.
- In `/mnt/t7/smad_next/PN1/fs_tree/pn1/data/manifests/train_*.csv`, every arm trains on `lad_R4` as a positive: A 132 rows, B 443, Sb 435.
- B and Sb also have `lad_R2w` (156) and `lad_half` (248) as negatives.
- Sb additionally has `lad_S3` (156) and `lad_S0` (156) negatives. These are same-person blends inside the face hull, which is the closest training analogue to R3.
- No arm has R3 itself.
- So the Sb − B gain on R4 vs R3 measures whether same-person splice negatives transfer to same-person whole-frame morphs. It does not measure a property learned without training on the contrast.

Replace the sentence at :490-491 with:
> "R3 (same-person whole-frame morph) is in no training list. R4 is a training positive in all three arms (A 132, B 443, Sb 435 rows on training hosts). B and Sb also have R2w and half negatives. Sb alone also has S3 and S0 same-person splice negatives, the nearest trained analogue of R3. An R4-vs-R3 gain of Sb over B therefore reads as transfer from Sb's same-person splice negatives to whole-frame same-person morphs. It does not show a contrast that no arm was trained toward."

At :519-520, change "on a contrast no arm was trained on" to "on a contrast whose negative class (R3) no arm was trained on".

**3. MAJOR: D needs a precise description, and the within-D floor is the one to print.**
- For morph rows, D is a per-host value taken from a different image: the S4 splice's D for R4 rows and the S3 splice's D for R3 rows (reader :151-156). It is a proxy for how different the two source faces are, not the morph's own blend energy, and it differs by class by construction.
  - Median log D on dev+clean80: R4 rows (S4 values) 2.59, R3 rows (S3 values) 2.28.
  - Rows per quintile: positives [15, 37, 33, 41, 54], negatives [57, 35, 39, 31, 18]. Every stratum is ≥ 10, so the read is readable.
- D alone barely separates R4 from R3: `pure_D_reference` is 0.572 in `floors/ri_R4_vs_R3_withinD.json` and 0.585 in `test_cv_R4_vs_R3_withinD.json`. Stratifying therefore costs little and removes little. Keeping the stratified statistic as the decider is consistent with NEXT rule v2, but the unstratified gain should have to agree.
- The reader prints `check.exceeds("ri_R4_vs_R3")`, which is the unstratified floor (0.79). The matching within-D floors are logreg 0.785 / 0.767 (all-cue) and 0.710 / 0.682 (nuisance). These floors come from a different set of rows (582 and 382 rows), so they are context only.

**5. MINOR: power is limited, and "absent" is nearly unreachable.**

- So "present" in practice needs a gain of about ≥ +0.05, and "absent" needs a seed-mean of about ≤ −0.02 (Sb no better than B).
