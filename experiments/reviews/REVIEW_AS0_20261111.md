## 1. Verdict
**Needs qualification.** The registered verdict "weak" is correct, and so are "real" and "not a method". "Clean" is over-stated:
- In the band that carries the effect (B3), the storage-history shift is at or above the registered 0.1 mark in every variant, and it is a fifth to a half of the identity effect.
- The ratio's level differs between photo pools by more than the identity effect.
- Geometry explains at least a fifth of the key contrast, and possibly more than half.
- The positive control is algebraic. It shows the code works, not that the statistic is sensitive.
- Conclusion 4 rests on the point estimate. The defensible statement is "not falsified".

**Recommendation: one more step, then close.** The step is the geometry-matched warp-only reference (R2w4) plus zero-CPU reads, less than 1 CPU-hour. Do not run the learned version before 14 Nov.

## 2. Findings, most severe first
**F1 (major): "barely moves with storage history" is not true in B3.** Evidence from history.csv and verdict.json, FaceMorpher E7 copies:
- Own landmarks: d = +0.102 [0.062, 0.151]. This fails the registered |d| < 0.1 itself, so B3 could not have passed even at 0.70. The shift is 0.21 of the gap between same-person and two-person morphs.
- Native landmarks: +0.204 (0.41 of the gap). Landmark-midline flip: +0.269. Blanked outside the mask: +0.160.
- Softened copies of the same ladder pool (Lsoft R2w fm61): +0.12 to +0.17.
- B4: 0.42 of the effect.
- The only clean band, B1 (−0.018), carries the weakest effect, 0.563.

Mitigation, which is a real strength: across all 296 history rows, no shift below the cut-off is more negative than −0.05. Stored and softened copies always move toward the genuine side. The ratio therefore cannot flag stored genuine photos (the failure found in the fixed-threshold re-read, EV1); it can only hide stored morphs. Softness likewise pushes against the morph direction, which strengthens "softness does not explain it".

**F2 (major): the pool offset is larger than the effect (reviewer, post hoc).** Genuine FRGC photos against genuine colorFERET photos (R0 against R0, AUC P(L_FRGC < L_FERET)):

| band | B1 | B2 | B3 | B4 | B5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AUC | 0.74 | 0.76 | 0.72 | 0.67 | 0.57 |

- In B3 the mean L gap is 0.41 nats, against a paired same-person-to-two-person gap of 0.225 nats.
- This also explains the pooled 0.84 of two-person morphs against R8 same-session morphs (tables.md:155); that row mixes databases and should not be quoted.
- The physics vetting's kill rule for a morph-free reference row (pool offset > 0.65, physics_vetting.md:356) would fire. Its evidence-row pass (pool offset ≤ 0.60, two-person against warp-only ≥ 0.70) fails on both counts: 0.72, and 0.690. These marks were never registered in AS0, so this is descriptive only.
- Consequence: the statistic is usable only as a within-pool comparison on the same hosts, never as one threshold across pools.

**F3 (major): geometry, "a quarter" is a lower bound.**
- Linear partial-out (tables.md:112) removes 19% of the above-chance AUC in B3, 34% in B2, 54% in B1 and 28% broadband. It uses proxies: a global shape scalar and three midline offsets. Imperfect proxies under-adjust.
- Detected shape asymmetry alone gives 0.590, against 0.618 for the pixel statistic.
- Symmetrising the shape alone (R2wm) removes 32% of the B3 energy ratio (shares.csv). The two-person shape halves its asymmetric energy (shape dose slope 0.84).
- So, to first order, the two-person morph's geometry alone lowers the ratio by about 15%. That is roughly 60% of the paired B3 log gap: ln 0.68 for R4 against R2w, versus ln 0.88 for R3 against R2w.
- Rewrite: "geometry explains at least a fifth (B3) to a half (B1); the geometry-matched reference (R2w4) decides how much image signal remains."

**F4 (moderate): the kill margin, and which numbers to quote.**
- verdict.json gives B2 = 0.5991, so result.md:20 "B2 and B3 are at or above 0.60" is wrong. Only B3 keeps the kill rule from firing: it is the best of five bands, with lower bound 0.580. The line between "weak" and "kill" is within noise.
- The intervals in result.md and README (B3 [0.578, 0.655]) are from the 19:22 run. The final values are B3 [0.580, 0.659] and B2 [0.561, 0.640].
- Multiplicity is mild: bands are correlated, the engines agree (0.625 / 0.611), and the person-disjoint halves agree. Quote the selection-free broadband figure 0.611 [0.573, 0.651] first.
- The effect is mostly an FRGC result: FRGC 0.634, colorFERET 0.581 [0.499, 0.661].

**F5 (moderate): session, i.e. whether 0.62 over- or under-states the identity part.**
- Every same-person morph (R3) is a cross-session mix of two captures, as is every two-person morph (R4). The capture-specific loss therefore appears in both arms and cancels to first order. The 0.62 is not inflated by session; it is the person-specific part, which includes shape.
- It understates for short gaps and fades with years: 0.642 at 60 days or less, 0.563 above 365 days (tables.md:86-88).
- The 0.69 against same-session same-person morphs (R8) is colorFERET only, best of five bands, 218 against 1,172 items, interval [0.61, 0.77], broadband 0.642. It adds capture averaging, which is 0.616 on its own (cross- against same-session). Do not quote it as the identity effect.

**F6 (moderate): the positive control is uninformative.** The mirror self-morph (R3m) was built with the same `cv2.flip` as the statistic's flip, so its collapse is algebra. That checks the code; it does not show sensitivity. The informative positive control is the dose curve: m = 0.5 against m = 0 gives 0.708 in B3 and is monotone, but shrinks at only 0.54 of the predicted rate.

**F7 (moderate): conclusion 4 does not follow as worded.**
- Detected typicality is 0.653 [0.616, 0.692] against a mark of 0.65. The defensible statement is "not falsified (lower bound above 0.60)". Detection keeps about 80% of the calculated margin (0.187 down to 0.153).
- Scope: clean 512 PNG landmark morphs whose tools warp to the exact mean shape. On GAN, detected shape asymmetry for two-person against same-person mixes is 0.515.
- Untested alternative: the detector misplaces landmarks more on blended images (error 0.0097 / 0.0121 against 0.0084 / 0.0094 for R3, README:225). Detected typicality on R2w4 discriminates.

**F8 (minor): conclusion 2's "baseline detectors must exceed" is a low bar.**
- On this same ladder, released detectors already score two-person against same-person morphs (R3 against R4) at 0.60 to 0.75 (AR1 result_tables.md:214-221): SelfMAD 0.66-0.69, MADation ViT-B/16 0.67-0.70, MADation ViT-L/14 0.74-0.75, MixFaceNet 0.60-0.62. Only MixFaceNet is near the 0.62 floor.
- On diffusion, the FZ1 floor of 0.681 inherits the same-person-mix artefact. Neutral and smiling FRLL photos have equal ratios (0.46 to 0.54 across B1-B5; reviewer, post hoc, corrected-MAD22 natives _03 against _08). The excess asymmetry is made by the diffusion mix, not present in the inputs.
- Against the reconstruction (two-person morph B20 against D20), asymmetry gives 0.60 and the FZ1 heads 0.62 to 0.63: a tie.

**F9 (minor): the look-alike explanation (README §8) is disfavoured.** Spearman of partner similarity (cos_HB) with L_R4 − L_R2w is −0.10 (p ≈ 0.02) in B2-B3, both engines. More similar partners lose slightly more asymmetry, not less.

**Conclusion 3 is sound.** GAN two-person against same-person mix is 0.48-0.52, and any two-code mix against the reconstruction is 0.61-0.68. Reconstruction against photo rises to 0.65, so the GAN inversion itself symmetrises.

## 5. Corrected wording for the notebook (mirror-asymmetry test, AS0)
**Result:** On the landmark ladder (536 hosts, both tools), a two-person morph has a lower left-right asymmetry ratio than a same-person morph of the same host, but weakly: 0.61 [0.57, 0.65] over all bands, 0.62 [0.58, 0.66] in the best of five; colorFERET alone 0.58 [0.50, 0.66]. Only the best band clears the kill mark of 0.60 (B2 0.599).
- At least a fifth of this, and possibly more than half, is face shape, which landmarks already give (detected shape asymmetry alone 0.59).
- About half of a photo's coarse-band asymmetry belongs to the capture, not the person.
- Storage history and softening move the ratio by up to 0.10 SD with own landmarks, and 0.10 to 0.27 SD in the best band under the other registered variants (a fifth to a half of the effect). The shift is always toward the genuine side, so the ratio cannot flag stored genuine photos.
- Its level differs between photo pools more than between morph types (genuine FRGC against genuine colorFERET 0.72; reviewer, post hoc).
- GAN: no two-person reading (0.48-0.52); any mix of two codes becomes more symmetric.
- Diffusion: the 0.74 against the same-person mix is mainly that mix being unusually asymmetric (its inputs are not); against the reconstruction, 0.60.
- Landmarks detected on the morph images keep most of the shape-typicality effect (0.653 [0.616, 0.692]; 0.687 from source files): not falsified.

**Verdict:** weak (registered). A history-stable, not history-free, physical fact about landmark morphs. Not a method, not a single-image detector, not generator-general. It is a floor that most released detectors already clear on this contrast.

**Next:** one CPU step against the geometry-matched warp-only reference (R2w4, amendment A14, registered before reading); then close.

**Paper wording:** "averaging two captures, and to a lesser degree two persons, lowers mirror asymmetry". Never "the ratio detects a second person".
