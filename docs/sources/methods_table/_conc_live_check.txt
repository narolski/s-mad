**What "shortcut" means here** (A41's definition, awaiting the author's sign-off). A detector's result on a benchmark is shortcut-carried when (i) some image property separates the benchmark's morphs from its genuine photos, (ii) the same property can be put into genuine photos without any morphing (here: downsampling the FRLL photos to the morphs' face size), and (iii) a control that removes the property, with everything else fixed, removes at least half of the separation. In numbers: the detector's EER on the benchmark is at most 10%, the person-clustered lower bound of its rise under the control is at least +10 points, and at least half of the separation is gone.

**Beside C2 (explanation, not part of "What we may write").** Post hoc (A42; one network, the same 204 photos; window shape pending the author's ruling): matching border area leaves the separation at 0.84. In the central 256-px window, which holds no zero-fill and which the loader enlarges about 2x, it falls to 0.61 [0.56, 0.65], while the rise against downsampled photos grows to +32.9 [28.9, 37.7]. **In plain words.**
- **C1.** The SelfMAD release treats genuine photos that were merely shrunk to the morphs' face size as more morph-like than real Greedy-DiM morphs: its error rate goes from 8% to 91%, so the ranking flips. The same happens on the raw benchmark files with the release's own face crop, with three different ways of shrinking the photos (descriptive). It also scores generator reconstructions of genuine photos, and photos that were only resized, above the published MorDIFF morphs. Morphs from a classic landmark tool (MAD22 FaceMorpher) stay detected under the same control, and a detector that ignores downsampling (the ViT-L/14 release) does not move. We do not know which image property it reads.
- **C2.** The ViT-B/16 release also gets worse against shrunk photos, in every crop we tried, but it does tell the published MorDIFF morphs from reconstructions of genuine photos, which our rules count against a shortcut. So we report its numbers and do not call it a shortcut.
- **C3.** No published detector we analysed ranks StyleGAN3 morphs above single-person StyleGAN3 renders by a useful margin; several rank them the other way round.
- **B and C4.** The benchmark morphs and genuine photos differ in how the files were made, and the GAN benchmarks compare generator PNGs with camera JPEGs.

**What amendment G2 added (no grade changed).**
- **The border test ran but could not be evaluated.** It splits the release's score change between native and shrunk photos into image regions and frequency bands, to see whether the warped border or the coarsest detail of our control photos carries it. Its two-point approximation explains only 38-48% of the SelfMAD release's score change (the rule needed it to within 20%), and for ViT-B/16 it even points the wrong way, so its parts mean nothing there. So a border or alignment artefact of our control pool is not formally excluded. For C1's main cell the border is small: it holds 8% of the pool's difference from the native photos and covers 0.2% of the release's input, so what stays open is mainly alignment and the MorDIFF cell. The test's fixed cuts would also have flagged untrained networks in 3 of 4 cells, so it could not have told us much either way.
- **The three downsampling kernels agree.** On the raw files, copies made with OpenCV's area filter, PIL bicubic and PIL Lanczos all separate from the originals almost perfectly (AUC 0.997, 0.999, 0.999), so the effect is not specific to one kernel. The two PIL kernels push the release further toward "morph" (about +1 on its logit scale; the error-rate rise grows by 4 to 21 points). All three targets use the same 204 photos at almost the same scale and sit at the ceiling, so this is one result, not three, and it does not say which property of the kernel matters.
- **Our forecasts were half right.** We predicted that the border test would fail for SelfMAD (it did) but work for ViT-B/16 (it did not), and that the kernels would agree on Greedy-DiM (they did) but not on MorDIFF (they agreed there too).

**Scope.** Each claim is about one released network (C1, C2) or the 12 rows we analysed (C3). C1's native regime and its reversal also hold on the raw files with the release's own crop (the reversal there is descriptive); its decision cells, and all of C2, are on our 512-px eye-aligned frames of FRLL-based pools. C3 is one generator (StyleGAN3). Every number is a single network, with intervals clustered by person over 102 FRLL persons; the crop test and the kernel ladder re-use the same persons and images, so neither is a replication. None of this says anything about MADMamba (sealed, not evaluated on H3), about W3 or about the FST. The earlier registered tests of the same questions said "neither" (C1: A32 H1; C2: A36 H-new-75; C3: A36 H-new-72) and are printed beside each row.

**Mechanism note (consistent with C1, not tested).** SelfMAD's released training code (our local copy at commit 673d064, not verified to be the code that trained the released weights) builds every self-morph training fake by blending a photo with a Lanczos-resampled zoom of itself, and never resamples the genuine partner; its RandomDownScale augmentation can hit either class. A network trained this way could learn to treat traces of resampling as a sign of a morph. That is consistent with C1; it does not explain it until a retrain without the interpolated zoom is run.

## What this means for the paper

**Shortcut claims about published detectors:** only the rows, grades and wording of "What we can claim about shortcuts" above, with its drop list ([A41](ledger:A41)).

**Can claim now, as selection data on a splice metric:** SBI as the largest lever, longer training once SBI is on, the frequency stream, stride 8, the core-v2 bundle (at E10's width and stride; A13 attributes most of it to v2's local convolution, descriptive), and the audit ([A8-W1F](ledger:A8-W1F), [A10-L](ledger:A10-L), [A10-M](ledger:A10-M)). Say that every adoption was selected on a splice-only metric (A10 plan 2.1) while the GAN diagnostic moved the other way. As a diagnostic only: E10 misses 30.9 ± 6.4%‡ of V-AMSL morphs at 1% BPCER on B2 (oracle threshold, n=6) ([A10-M](ledger:A10-M)). Describe the frequency stream as an input E10 cannot do without, not as an auxiliary branch; the knockout is a diagnostic, never a detector ([A19](ledger:A19)).

**Do not claim that selective scanning, long memory or image-wide dependencies are why MADMamba works.** Its gains come from a frequency stream, finer tokens and a bundle that includes a 2D local convolution; a CNN could use all three. MADMamba with every scan replaced by its skip term matched it on selection data twice and on held-out diffusion morphs (both near chance). Put this ablation in the main results with its GAN columns, and say the scan's only measurable role is on GAN-type morphs, where E10 still ranks 5th of six L0 entries. By PROTOCOL §5 Q2 the scan would have been pruned; disclose that the test came after the freeze. The title should not credit selective state spaces or the untested content gate (Decision 5).

**Why L0 matters.** Pretraining reaches about 1% here, so a from-scratch detector needs a reason: SYN-MAD 2022 trains on SMDD only (check huber2022synmad on pretrained weights).
