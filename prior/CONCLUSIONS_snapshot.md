- **No detector ranks GAN morphs above single-person renders made by the same generator at AUC 0.65 or more:** this holds for our 84 networks and, under A41's registered one-sided rule, for the 12 published-method rows (highest row AUC 0.59, upper bound 0.62). A simple linear read-out fitted on these very images does separate them (A38), so the information is in the features, unused by the detectors. But in this test a morph's latent code is a midpoint of two, so it is less spread out than a render's, and that spread alone separates the two classes perfectly (A39). On single-person renders matched to the morphs' spread, all 84 of our networks are at chance (A37, descriptive), and so is the read-out: it scores these renders as morph-like as the morphs (morphs vs these renders AUC 0.45 and 0.49 for CLIP and ConvNeXt features; these vs ordinary renders 0.95 and 0.93), so its whole separation came from how average a face's latent code is, not from two identities (A39 P, H-new-101 supported). This makes plain H3 an unfair test of identity mixing, because its morphs are simply more average. The spread-matched version is fair on averageness, but it cannot show that a detector lacks identity evidence either: for a morph made by averaging two latent codes, a matched single-person render is almost the same kind of image by construction ([A30](ledger:A30), [A36](ledger:A36), [A37](ledger:A37), [A38](ledger:A38), [A39](ledger:A39)).

## What we set out to do

We want a detector that says whether one face photo is a morph of two people. MADMamba, our candidate, is a Mamba network trained from scratch: it reads the image as a sequence of patches with a running memory, plus a frequency stream for fine detail. Does it beat standard CNN and transformer baselines in a fair, pre-registered comparison?

The rules: hypotheses come first; every number is one network, mean ± SD over seeds; choices use only selection data; the claim is tested once, after the freeze, on a new sealed test set (FST). An audit (A8) overturned the original comparison (E00-E11); two fair grids followed (A9, A10). A12's side studies cannot change the detector ([A12-BR](ledger:A12-BR), [A12-SP](ledger:A12-SP)).

- **The first comparison did not favour MADMamba** (32.63 ± 0.22%, n=3, tied with Swin-T) ([E01](ledger:E01)). Earlier, better numbers rested on bona fide resolution differing from the morphs' ([E07](ledger:E07)). That shortcut was not specific to the frequency stream, but the old stream amplified it ([E07](ledger:E07), [E02](ledger:E02)).
- **Statistics.** E-series "three-seed means" were fused 3-network ensembles; per network, one such configuration scored 24.39 ± 8.82% (n=3) ([E10](ledger:E10)). 2,178 of 5,033 "held-out" bona fide images were training captures ([E03](ledger:E03)). The scans were scrambled, so scan order was never tested ([E02](ledger:E02)).

### Inside MADMamba: D-A10c closed the search

- **The entry is E10**, the wide stride-8 `mm2_t_v2_conv_v2_p8` at 384 px, 36k: 8.60 ± 1.17% (n=6), -3.96 [-5.62, -2.39] against the previous entry E3 (12.56 ± 1.47). D-A10c kept neither pruning twin ([A10-M](ledger:A10-M)).
- **The frequency stream helps where we selected, and costs on MIPGAN (from scratch only):** +16.71 [+14.49, +18.92] at E10 on the selection metric, in both FERET (+14.75‡) and FRGC (+20.79‡), and +10.5 AUC on AMSL. On diffusion it only reduces the penalty on downsampled bona fide (native pool −1.6); it costs MIPGAN-I (−11 AUC) and MIPGAN-II (−4.7), not FRLL StyleGAN, and deepens the G-test inversion. With an ImageNet backbone it has no effect above 2 points. Morph type and native resolution cannot be separated in these sets ([A10-M](ledger:A10-M), [A18](ledger:A18)). E10 cannot work without its stream: with the stream removed at inference, its 16 networks are near chance on the selection set (8.97 → 43.14%), twice the cost of training without it (+32 vs +17 points on the A10 seeds). So whether its MIPGAN cost could be undone at inference is not determined, and about half of the loss is E10's weights relying on the stream ([A19](ledger:A19)).
- **The stream's advantage is not the training-time processing shortcut, on AMSL-type morphs (A22, exploratory, post-freeze).** Trained with the same randomised resampling / JPEG / blur on both classes, E10 keeps its full lead over its no-stream twin on the selection metric (+18.10 [+16.79, +19.46] vs +16.08 without the augmentation, 6 paired seeds) and on FRLL AMSL (+19.2 AUC), while its different treatment of downsampled bona fide disappears (+14.5 → −0.2). The lead holds because the no-stream twin degrades more, so it does not "grow". Other landmark tools show no stream effect. The MIPGAN-I cost persists (−13.1 AUC), and the G-test stays inverted in every arm. The small diffusion advantage near chance (+6.6 AUC) is not seen with the augmentation (descriptive) ([A22](ledger:A22)).
- **The AMSL advantage also survives test-time degradation** (A25, zero GPU, descriptive): +6.7 to +15.0 EER points on FRLL AMSL under every E5 variant; the other landmark families are near 0, and the GAN cost grows under JPEG (PROTOCOL A25 (1)).
- **On pretrained Mamba backbones the stream shows no detectable effect** (A26; VMamba-T n=6, MambaVision-B-21K n=10): GAN group −1.03 [−4.30, +2.20] and +1.59 [−0.37, +3.74] EER points, AMSL uninformative (MambaVision SD 17.8), with no harm on the selection set, where both are already near 0%. MambaVision's GAN-family readings point in opposite directions (FRLL StyleGAN worse, G-test better; confounded). MambaVision numbers: NVIDIA Source Code License-NC ([A26](ledger:A26)).
- **The proposed training method (a22n + our GAN morphs) is not a confirmed gain** (A30; four pretrained backbones x arms a / b / c, n=6). On the design-seen diffusion group, adding our GAN morphs lowered EER for CLIP LoRA (−11.8 points) and HRNet (−35.8), but HRNet's baseline was worse than chance, and an eye-geometry-only classifier trained on our GAN morphs (21.6%) beats every network (best 27.1%). The GAN-group gains for CLIP and ConvNeXt come from the G-test, our own StyleGAN recipe, not from the held-out MIPGAN-I. a22n alone harms the GAN group (G-test) for three backbones and costs 0.4-1.5 VALX-A* points. Held out, a22n repairs inverted errors on MIPGAN-I-style morphs (H4b: EER 36-59 → 17-27) and the GAN data help on H4 pp, but no network beats the sets' file-size floors, and every network stays at chance on diffusion identity mixing (Arc2Face, H2). MambaVision numbers: NVIDIA Source Code License-NC ([A30](ledger:A30)).
- **On a clean GAN test, no network tells a morph from a single-person render** (A30, H3, 84 networks, exploratory, post-freeze). Both classes come from StyleGAN3, a generator we never trained on (423 pairs). The best arm reaches AUC 63.6 on unprocessed images, not reliably above what pose, file size and borders give (60.7). After mild post-processing every network is at or below chance. Every network flags 91-100% of the single-person renders as morphs, but most flag under 10% of 204 FRLL camera photos (range 0-35%). The weak signal does not grow when the two people look less alike. So on GAN images these networks separate generator output from our camera photos, with no sign of reading identity mixing. Even arm a, which never saw a GAN morph in training, flags 99% of the renders. MambaVision: NVIDIA NC ([A30](ledger:A30)).
- **a22n removes false alarms on downsampled photos** (A30 amendment 6, descriptive, post hoc). On the same 2,000 FFHQ photos it cuts the flag rate of their downsampled copies from 19-40% (arm a) to 4-8% (arms b and c) in all four backbones; the original-resolution copies stay at 16-50%. Our GAN morphs without a22n add 14-16 points of false alarms on the original photos. This is the training method's first held-out benefit that bears on a known shortcut ([A30](ledger:A30)).
- **The render flag survives a frame-matched camera control only without a threshold** (A30 amendment 6). Every arm still ranks the StyleGAN3 renders above FFHQ camera photos of the same framing and face size (AUC 0.93-0.99; 0.85-0.93 after JPEG post-processing), but at the selection threshold no network is specific to renders. What drives it (stored fine detail, source, pose, a generic out-of-domain response) is not identified ([A30](ledger:A30)).
- **Within each H3 class no simple covariate explains the scores** (A30 amendment 6). Scores rise weakly with a render's distance from the generator's mean latent, but not with its distance from the inversions' mean, which is what separates morphs from renders. So "morphs look more average, hence real" does not explain why the networks rank renders above morphs ([A30](ledger:A30)).
- **Matching latent spread removes every network's StyleGAN3 morph signal** (A37, 84 networks, one generator, exploratory, post-freeze). All 84 networks passed the integrity checks. On identical images, scores differed between jobs by up to 0.015 in CLIP networks, and no AUC or flag rate moved by more than 0.0002. On renders matched to the morphs' latent spread, every arm scores the morphs at or below the renders (AUC 42.8-49.2; read 'at chance': the matched renders are slightly more average than the morphs, A39 P review). The two arms above chance on H3 (CN g 63.6, CL g 57.1) fall to 46.5 and 46.4. H-new-88 is 'neither' (one arm short of support): spread moves arm b 2-7 points toward chance and leaves arm c unchanged. Truncated renders score far below both classes (H-new-81 'overshoot'), but simple image properties separate them at AUC 0.96, so this does not show typicality. Arm a still flags 96-99% of renders whose fine texture was removed (H-new-89 falsified). Their scores fall somewhat, so fine texture contributes but does not carry the flag ([A37](ledger:A37)).
- **Finer tokens help, on the splice metric.** Stride 8 lowered EER at both widths, e.g. narrow 12.56 → 10.01% (-2.55 [-4.24, -1.06]). Width alone gave no reliable gain ([A10-M](ledger:A10-M)).
- **The core result is not evidence for the SSM; most of it is a 2D convolution.** With only v2's local convolution, the core-v1 network ties E10 on the point estimate (+0.69 [−0.54, +1.90]; +0.08 against E10's seeds 4-6), gains −3.85 [−5.26, −2.40] over the twin, and keeps about 3.6 tokens of SSM memory at the fine stages. With only v2's Delta it does not reach E10 (+3.53 [+1.93, +5.06]); whether the Delta adds anything is unclear (−1.02 [−2.39, +0.62]), as in A8 ([A13-BA](ledger:A13-BA), [A8-W1B](ledger:A8-W1B)). A13.3 and A13.5 then removed the scan from this network: 7.97% without the state term, 8.44% without token mixing, against 9.29% with the scan (n=6 each). No reading shows a cost, and both train in 0.6× the time. This is the convolution-only cell, not E10 ([A13-3](ledger:A13-3), [A13-5](ledger:A13-5)).
- **Run noise, not batches.** 15 same-seed re-runs differ by 1.32 points (SD) and do not track their originals (r = -0.06); batches vary no more than re-runs (F = 0.52). Each 36k run carries about 1 point of training noise. A13's 0.97 "twin shift" was regression to the mean (post hoc, [A13-3](ledger:A13-3)).
- **Every network reacts almost perfectly to SBI's echo** (probe AUC 0.95-1.00), so probes cannot rank families ([A10-D](ledger:A10-D)).
- **Cost.** E10 has 21.05M parameters, E3 9.47M ([A10-M](ledger:A10-M)), EfficientNet-B4 17.6M, HRNet-W18 19.3M ([A10-W](ledger:A10-W)).

## How sure we are

- **Selection data, not results.** VALX-A\* holds out people from the two training collections (FERET, FRGC) and one splice tool, not new datasets or morph kinds. We chose on it, so chosen numbers are optimistic, MADMamba's most ([A10-M](ledger:A10-M)).
- **MADMamba trails the three CNNs in both FERET and FRGC‡**, and on seeds 4-6 trails HRNet-W18 by 2.2 points, not 1.2 ([A10-M](ledger:A10-M)).
- **The halves differ.** FRGC morphs can be told from FRGC bona fide by smoothness alone (A9 plan F11); EfficientNet-B4's lead and the core-v2 gain sit mostly in FRGC ([A10-M](ledger:A10-M)).
- **W3 numbers are descriptive,** on sets used during design, with no CI or test. All seven diffusion families share one 204-image bona fide pool. A detector's result on a benchmark is shortcut-carried when (i) some image property separates the benchmark's morphs from its genuine photos, (ii) the same property can be put into genuine photos without any morphing (here: downsampling the FRLL photos to the morphs' face size), and (iii) a control that removes the property, with everything else fixed, removes at least half of the separation. - **The three downsampling kernels agree.** On the raw files, copies made with OpenCV's area filter, PIL bicubic and PIL Lanczos all separate from the originals almost perfectly (AUC 0.997, 0.999, 0.999), so the effect is not specific to one kernel. The two PIL kernels push the release further toward "morph" (about +1 on its logit scale; the error-rate rise grows by 4 to 21 points). All three targets use the same 204 photos at almost the same scale and sit at the ceiling, so this is one result, not three, and it does not say which property of the kernel matters.
- **Our forecasts were half right.** We predicted that the border test would fail for SelfMAD (it did) but work for ViT-B/16 (it did not), and that the kernels would agree on Greedy-DiM (they did) but not on MorDIFF (they agreed there too).

**Scope.** Each claim is about one released network (C1, C2) or the 12 rows we analysed (C3). C1's native regime and its reversal also hold on the raw files with the release's own crop (the reversal there is descriptive); its decision cells, and all of C2, are on our 512-px eye-aligned frames of FRLL-based pools. C3 is one generator (StyleGAN3). Every number is a single network, with intervals clustered by person over 102 FRLL persons; the crop test and the kernel ladder re-use the same persons and images, so neither is a replication. None of this says anything about MADMamba (sealed, not evaluated on H3), about W3 or about the FST. The earlier registered tests of the same questions said "neither" (C1: A32 H1; C2: A36 H-new-75; C3: A36 H-new-72) and are printed beside each row.

**Mechanism note (consistent with C1, not tested).** SelfMAD's released training code (our local copy at commit 673d064, not verified to be the code that trained the released weights) builds every self-morph training fake by blending a photo with a Lanczos-resampled zoom of itself, and never resamples the genuine partner; its RandomDownScale augmentation can hit either class. **Do not claim that selective scanning, long memory or image-wide dependencies are why MADMamba works.** Its gains come from a frequency stream, finer tokens and a bundle that includes a 2D local convolution; a CNN could use all three. **After W3:** 10-seed results, "seen" benchmark and G-test numbers, and a SYN-MAD comparison through the d0 twins; none needs the FST. If the FST exists: the registered test (α 0.025, all five baselines beaten) ([A9-W3](ledger:A9-W3)).

**The registered test is very unlikely to pass.**

- With 10 seeds per arm it has 80% power only for gaps of about 1.48 seed SDs (about 1.7 points at E10's SD), before image noise ([A9-W3](ledger:A9-W3)).

## Glossary

- **Morph, bona fide.** A morph blends two faces into one photo; a bona fide image is a genuine photo of one person.
- **EER.** The error rate at which missed morphs and wrongly flagged bona fide images are equally common.
- **BPCER, APCER.** The share of bona fide images flagged, and of morphs missed, at a fixed threshold.
- **Mamba, SSM.** Reads patches as a sequence with an input-dependent running memory (selective state space); core v1 and v2 are versions of MADMamba's.
- **E3, E10.** MADMamba's narrow stride-16 entry and its wide stride-8 entry. Ledger entry [E10](ledger:E10) is unrelated.
- **League.** Outside data allowed. L0: none. L1: ImageNet-1k. L2: larger pretraining. L3: distilled from a teacher.
- **VALX-A\*.** Our selection metric: EER on a held-out splice tool and held-out people, weighted to the morphs' FERET/FRGC mix. VALX-A is unweighted.
- **GAN diagnostic.** GAN morphs whose bona fide images are not resolution-matched ("unmatched"); reported, never decisive.
- **Splice-type morph, SBI.** A splice pastes a blended inner face onto one photo. SBI makes training morphs by blending a face with an altered copy of itself.
- **Shortcut floor, guards.** The floor: a detector on 26 low-level image statistics. Guards reject choices that lean on known shortcuts.
- **Echo, probe.** SBI leaves a faint shifted copy of the image. A probe adds one SBI artefact to real photos and checks whether scores move toward "morph".
- **Freeze, W3, FST.** Selection stops at the freeze; W3 retrains each frozen setting with 10 fresh seeds for the FST, a new sealed test set.
- **SMDD, d0 twin.** A synthetic morph training set; a d0 twin trains on it alone, for the SYN-MAD comparison.
- **A12 terms.** R / P: a method's published recipe / our protocol. d0 / d1: SMDD only / our data. BENCH: MAD22 and FRLL-Morphs. UA8c: held-out diffusion morphs (six DiM variants, MorDIFF).
